# Getting started in AstroWars

You just joined a round. This page is the short version: what the game is about, what to do
in your first hour and your first days, and what the jargon means. It is deliberately
incomplete. When you want depth, the [full player guide](player-guide.md) and the exact
numbers in [game-rules.md](game-rules.md) are one click away.

## What the game is

AstroWars is a slow strategy game played in a browser. You own a home planet, grow it, claim
more planets, build a fleet, and fight over space with other players. A round lasts days
(on the **RedZone** server, about ten times faster, so hours instead of days).

Nothing happens in a hurry. Population grows every hour, buildings and research take time,
and fleets take hours to fly. You do not need to sit at the screen. Checking in a few times a
day is how most people play, and **making a good choice and then leaving is normal**.

Players work in **alliances**. Alone you are a target; with an alliance you get shared
defence, trade partners and someone to ask. If you are here through RAID, you already have
one.

## The loop

Everything you do is some version of this:

1. **Produce** points on your planets (population and Robotic Factories make production
   points every hour).
2. **Spend** them on buildings, ships and research.
3. **Grow** your culture, which raises how many planets you may own.
4. **Colonize** new planets with Colony Ships.
5. Repeat, and defend what you have built.

## Your first hour

- **Look at where you spawned.** You start on one planet with two empty ("Free") planets
  beside it. If the spot looks bad (next to a big strong alliance, far from your own
  allies), you have about **30 minutes** to relog and reroll. After that you are stuck there
  for a long time.
- **Pick your race traits.** Unsure? Leave everything at zero (`0/0/0/0/0/0/0`). It is a
  perfectly good way to learn. If you do spend points, avoid **-4 Production**, **-4 Attack**
  and **-4 Defense** (each one hurts you badly while you are new). Growth is the safest
  trait to drop.
- **Start your build queue** (next section) and **start a research.** Production points that
  sit unspent are wasted.

## Your first days

**Build order.** The usual opening is *Robotic Factory, then Galactic Cybernet, then
Hydroponic Farm, then Research Lab.* Production first, because farms and labs are only
useful once you can afford the rest of your queue. The classic mistake is building the farm
or lab too early and starving everything else.

**Research.** One science at a time. The six, in plain terms:

| Science | What it does for you |
|---|---|
| Biology | See further on the map |
| Economy | Cheaper ships |
| Energy | Faster fleets |
| Mathematics | Fewer losses when attacked |
| Physics | Better odds when you attack |
| Social | Higher population cap on your planets |

If a Hydroponic Farm shows **+0 growth**, nothing is broken. You have hit your population cap
for your current Social level, and more Social raises it.

**Expand.** Each culture level gives you one more planet slot. Fill it by sending a Colony
Ship to a Free planet. Send one ship and you get an empty planet. Send extra ships and the
spares turn into starting production, which gives the colony a real head start.

**Artifacts.** You can run one at a time. Early on, take a **culture** one (Basalt Monolith,
or Crystal Rod for culture plus growth) so your planet count keeps climbing.

## Staying safe

- **Do not send fleets alone through contested space.** Colony Ships and Transports that get
  intercepted cost you the ships and the time.
- **Keep your fleet together.** Splitting it usually means several fights you lose instead of
  one you would have won.
- **Check before you fight.** The hub's Battle Calculator tells you the likely outcome.
  Skipping a fight you were unsure about is almost always the right call.
- **Tiny fleets (under 4 ships) can be wiped** even in a fight they technically win.
- **Planets keep growing while your fleet is in the air.** When you send Transports to take a
  planet, count its population **on arrival**, not at launch.

## Using the hub

The sidebar buttons open the tools. The ones most useful to a new player:

- **Build Order**: what to build next.
- **Travel Calc** and **Route Planner**: how long a flight takes and which route is fastest.
- **Battle Calc**: estimate a fight before you commit.
- **Galaxy Map**: where everyone is.

Tools open only when you press their button, so they never cover the game. Everything else
in the sidebar can wait until you are curious. You can hide any tool you do not use under
**Settings**.

## Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Alliance** | Your team. Shares score, defence and trade. |
| **A$** | The game's money, used for Trade Agreements. |
| **CV** | Combat value, how strong a fleet is in a fight. |
| **Culture** | A level that sets how many planets you can own. |
| **Free planet** | An empty planet anyone can colonize. |
| **NAP** | Non-Aggression Pact. An informal promise not to attack each other. Backed by reputation, not by the game. |
| **PP** | Production points, what you spend on buildings, ships and research. |
| **RZ** | RedZone, the fast server (about 10x pace). Some numbers differ there. |
| **SB** | Starbase, a defensive and vision building on a planet. |
| **TA** | Trade Agreement. Boosts your economy bonuses. Costs A$ and needs a partner. |
| **Transport** | A non-combat ship that captures or bombs a populated planet. |
